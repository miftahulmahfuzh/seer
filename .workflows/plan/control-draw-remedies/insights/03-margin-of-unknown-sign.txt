This supersedes nothing and confirms two rows already in this journal, from a direction
neither of them could see.

The paper-gains book's lead over a meaningless book was published as "at least 4.3 points a
year - provisional, and a floor rather than a measurement". The word "floor" was doing real
work and it had an argued mechanism behind it: the only opponent the lab could draw at the
time traded a quarter less often than the book, so it under-paid the broker; a correctly
busy opponent would pay the full fee bill, earn less, and the true lead could therefore only
be WIDER than 4.3.

The correctly busy opponent has now been drawn. It trades 596 times against the old
opponent's 455 and the book's 624, and it matches the book on both axes. It earned 10.1% a
year against the old opponent's 9.5%. It earned MORE, not less. The lead is 3.7 points -
BELOW the floor.

The mechanism is measured false. On this book, how much a blind draw happens to earn varies
by more than the whole fee difference between 455 and 596 trades. The fee argument was
sound as far as it went; it was simply the smaller of the two effects, and the larger one
has no sign.

Two rows in this journal reached the same conclusion from the opposite end: an under-trading
control is a cheaper strategy rather than a shape-matched one, and an over-trading control's
handicap is arbitrary rather than a fee drag of known sign. That second one was drawn from
thirteen arms across nine books. This is a fourteenth, on the under-trading side, and it is
the first time the prediction was written down before the run and then scored.

The rule to carry forward:

    A margin measured against a control that does not match its book's shape is an estimate
    of unknown sign. It is not a floor, it is not a ceiling, and it is not conservative.

So never write "at least" in front of such a number. Write the number, say which axis the
control misses and by how much, and say plainly that the true figure may be larger or
smaller. If a sentence already in the lab calls a control-shape caveat a floor or a bound,
it is suspect by this argument and should be re-read.
