## Detailed Requirements Understanding

**Problem statement.** `walkforward.pick` ranks a method's registered variants by MAR on each
fold's training slice and scores the winner on the unseen evaluation slice. It ranks whatever
mapping its caller hands it. Both callers — `hardgate.fold_record` (via `_dev_curves`) and
`commands/lab.py:_walkforward` — hand it **every** dev variant, controls included. The audit asks
whether that is a measurement error.

**Success criteria.** A count, not an opinion: how often a control is actually chosen, and how
often that choice changes the gate's answer. The decisive number is the second one, because a
selector that picks an odd arm but reaches the same verdict has cost nothing.

**Key constraint.** Everything is read from recorded curves. No store, no backtest, no look, no
method id, no write. The live `lab/lab.sqlite` was **copied to the scratchpad** and every read ran
against the copy, so the live file could not be migrated-on-connect even by accident.

**Verification of the extraction.** `lab walkforward M0179 M0174 M0178 M0184 M0202` was run
against the copy and matches the extraction row for row (1 of 4 / −0.6%, 1 of 4 / −2.1%,
2 of 4 / +0.3%, 0 of 4 / −4.8%, 2 of 4 / +4.5%). Live DB sha1 `f2ff9ce6…` before and after.

## Current Dataflow

### Entry Point: `hardgate.check(conn, method_id)`

**Location:** `engine/src/seer_engine/lab/hardgate.py:1749`
**Trigger:** `commands/lab.py:_promote`, before `prereg.promote_method`.
**Clauses, in order:** every-fold-scoreable → `MIN_FOLDS` → **picks' majority (D11a)** →
**promoted variant's own majority (D11b)** → survivorship drawdown (D16) → control shape (D17) →
kin (K/D4/D12/D14). There is no era-edge clause in `check()`; the era edge lives only in the buy
signal.

### The selector

1. `hardgate._dev_curves` (`:850`) — `SELECT … WHERE window='dev' AND method_id=?`. **No role
   filter**, documented deliberately: *"Every variant is a candidate, eligible or not, exactly as
   `lab walkforward` does it: the fold's question is 'which variant would the lab's own rule have
   named at this point in time', and that rule ranks the whole family of variants, not a
   pre-filtered subset."*
2. `walkforward.pick` (`walkforward.py:176`) — `max` by training-slice MAR over `sorted(curves)`,
   ties on candidate id.
3. `walkforward.evaluate` — scores the chosen curve on the unseen slice against `REF-SPY-HOLD`.

### The rule the selector is simulating

`store.best_dev_eligible` (`store.py:1234`) — `ORDER BY mar DESC, n ASC` over **all** the method's
dev trials, returning the first whose `verdict(...).eligible`. **It has no role filter either.**
So the selector and the real promotion rule agree about the candidate set; the selector differs
only in that it cannot apply the eligibility filter (profit factor and trade count cannot be
sliced from a monthly curve — stated in `walkforward.py`'s docstring).

### Fold geometry (from the recorded `REF-SPY-HOLD` curve)

| fold | train ends | evaluates | methods beating SPY |

| 1 | 2003-01-01 | 2003-01..2005-12 | 50 of 167 (30%) |

## Findings

### F1 — the selector picks a control often (R1)

Of the **44** kin-clean fold-refused dev-eligible methods (the handover said 45; see *Population*
below):

| | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| a control arm chosen in **≥1 fold** | **24** | 55% |
| a control arm chosen in **every fold** | **9** | 20% |
| role of all 176 picks | 104 treatment · **62 control** · 10 dose | |

The nine scored entirely on a control: M0058, M0083, M0088, M0169, M0174, M0178, **M0179**,
M0187, M0193.

**The pre-registered prediction was wrong, and is not revised.** It said *"fewer than a third of
the 45, because most lab methods register more treatment arms than control arms."* The measured
answer is 55%, not under 33%. The reasoning behind the prediction was sound (treatment arms do
outnumber control arms, 595 to 189 across the lab) but it missed that a control arm is
*systematically* the lowest-turnover, lowest-drawdown arm, and MAR rewards exactly that.

**M0179 confirmed verbatim.** All four folds picked `M0179-P20-SW` — *"Control: the 20 most
positive-coskewness members, same band and switch"* — while the method's idea is the most
*negative* coskewness. The recorded analysis (*"Every time that was the control, never the
idea"*) is exactly right.

**M0230 is not a second case.** The handover records it as one; it is not. M0230's picks were
`M0230-C30` (a dose-response rung) in folds 1-2 and `M0230-C50-SWS` (a treatment arm) in folds
3-4. It is also `rejected`, so it was never in the 45. One documented case, not two.

### F2 — but it almost never changes the verdict (R2)

Re-scoring all 44 with the selector constrained to treatment arms only (`first` pinned to the full
set's minimum so the restriction is the only thing that moves):

- picks' record **unchanged**: 31 of 44
- picks' record **moved**: 9
- picks' record reaches a majority: 5
- **the complete gate verdict flips: 2**

| flip | picks | restricted | own variant (D11b) | 2009-15 era edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M0174** | 1 of 4 | **4 of 4** | `M0174-C40-SW` 3 of 4 ✔ | **−0.6%** (promoted arm: +0.5%) |
| **M0178** | 1 of 4 | **3 of 4** | `M0178-L40-SW` 3 of 4 ✔ | **−2.1%** (promoted arm: −2.1%) |
| M0179 | 2 of 4 | 3 of 4 | `M0179-N40-SW` 2 of 4 ✘ | +0.3% (promoted arm: −5.6%) |
| M0184 | 0 of 4 | 3 of 4 | `M0184-T40-SW` 2 of 4 ✘ | −4.8% (promoted arm: −4.8%) |
| M0202 | 2 of 4 | 3 of 4 | `M0202-PLC1-HALF` 2 of 4 ✘ | +4.5% (promoted arm: −1.2%) |

Three of the five are still refused by D11b — the clause requiring the variant that actually gets
the look and the money to have won its own folds. **D11 is already doing the job (A) proposes.**

**The restriction is not monotone.** M0107 goes 2 of 4 → **1 of 4**: removing its controls removes
the arms that were winning. A constrained selector is not a corrected measurement, it is a
different one with its own arbitrary losers.

**Four methods have no treatment arm at all** — M0187 and M0193 are entirely hash-order placebo
calibrations; M0260 and M0261 are entirely dose-response ladders. (A)'s fix is undefined for them.

### F3 — the 2-of-4 pile-up is the era, not the selector

Across all 167 methods with four scoreable folds, the win pattern (T = beat SPY):

| pattern | methods | |
|---|---|---|
| `.T..` | 60 (36%) | won the crash only |
| `.T.T` | 40 (24%) | |
| `TT.T` | 28 (17%) | |
| `TT..` | 11 (7%) | |
| everything else | 28 (17%) | |

Fold 2 (2006-2008) is won by 88% of the lab and fold 3 (2009-2011) by 10%. Nearly every lab book
sits behind the same SPY 200-day gate, which wins the famous crashes and misses the rebound — the
same shape already recorded as *"the gate's best years are the famous crashes"*. No choice of arm
moves a fold that 90% of the lab loses. **"2 of 4" is the lab's modal outcome and the signature of
a shared gate, not a near miss.**

### F4 — the real defect is one clause downstream: `best_dev_eligible` can pre-register a control

`store.best_dev_eligible` ranks all variants by MAR with no role filter, and it is what
`prereg.promote_method` calls. Measured over the 78 dev-eligible methods, the role of the variant
`lab promote` **would pre-register**:

| role | count |
|---|---|
| treatment | 46 |
| **control** | **19** |
| dose-response | 9 |
| (none eligible) | 4 |

**19 of 78 would spend the counted look and the owner's money on an arm that exists to be
beaten.** M0202's would be `M0202-PLC1-HALF` — *"Placebo: the same scoreable names in a fixed hash
order (seed 1)"*. M0044's would be `M0044-NOBRAKE`, M0035's `M0035-CTRL`, M0215's `M0215-CTRL`.

Three of the 19 — M0033, M0035, M0044 — already clear **both** D11 fold clauses at 3 of 4 and are
stopped only by the kin rule. That is luck, not design: the next method of this shape with clean
kin is promotable today.

**Which 78 — the population matters, and two defensible counts differ.** This figure is over
`methods.status = 'dev-eligible'` (78), because `check()` opens with
`if str(row["status"]) != _GATED_STATUS: return` and `store.TRANSITIONS` admits only
dev-eligible → promoted. Counting instead every method for which `best_dev_eligible` returns a
row gives 86 and 21 controls — but the extra 12 (M0012, M0021, M0022, M0029, M0075, M0077,
M0156, M0161, M0176, M0180, M0203, M0213) are **9 `rejected` and 3 `test-failed`**, and no clause
in the gate can ever reach them. **19 of 78 is the number a claim about the gate may use; 21 of
86 would assert the gate might pre-register a control arm for a method that has already failed
its test look.** (The gap the other way — M0007, M0011, M0019, M0020 — are dev-eligible with no
trial the current gate and N call eligible; they are the four `(none)` above, and `check()` is
already silent for them.)

This is the audit's question (*is the gate scoring the idea or its control?*) asked of the clause
that actually spends the money, and there the answer is **yes, and it is not guarded**.

### F5 — the era-edge column reports a different arm from the one that would be promoted (R4)

`commands/lab.py:2620` picks `best` = the arm with the highest **2009-2015 MAR** — chosen with
hindsight over that very era, from all arms including controls — and reports *its* CAGR lead as
the `2009-15 edge`. That value is passed straight to `walkforward.buy_signal` as
`high_coverage_edge`, the 4th buy condition.

**M0179's loose thread, resolved.** Both numbers are correct and they are about different arms:

| source | arm | role | 2009-2015 edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lab walkforward` `+0.3%` | `M0179-N20` | treatment | **+0.32 pts** (highest era MAR) |
| `lab survivorship` `−5.6%` dev | `M0179-N40-SW` | the **promoted** variant | **−5.57 pts** (measured here) |
| `lab survivorship` `−3.7%` honest | `M0179-N40-SW` on `.research-sv` | | recorded in insight 499 |

`lab survivorship`'s *"the numbers below are for its best version"* means `best_dev_eligible` —
the promoted variant. `lab walkforward`'s column means the hindsight-best arm on the era itself.
**The buy-signal condition, as implemented, means the walkforward one.**

Read from insight 499 in the committed record; `lab survivorship` was **not** re-run (the handover
warns that journal 500 is already a duplicate of 499 from exactly that mistake).

**How load-bearing.** Over the 74 dev-eligible methods where both are measurable:

- the reported edge is a **different arm** from the promoted one: **39 (53%)**
- the reported edge is a **control arm**: **19**
- the two **disagree on sign**: **18** — 11 report positive while the promoted arm is negative,
  and 7 the reverse (so it is not merely conservative; it can also suppress a true positive)
- worst case: **M0201 reports +1.82 pts on `M0201-PLC2-MV10`, a placebo, while its promoted arm
  `M0201-Y20-MV10-SW` is at −7.82 pts** — a 9.6-point gap across a sign boundary

**CORRECTED — the exposure is live, not forward.** An earlier draft of this section said the
signal had never fired and that every past "no buy signal" statement stood. **That was wrong**,
and it was wrong because it generalised a five-method `lab walkforward` run to the whole lab
without checking. Run over all 168 methods against the copy, the signal **fires today on four**:

```
  *** BUY SIGNAL ***
  M0092: cleared all 4: dev-eligible, kin clean, 3 of 4 folds, 2009-2015 edge +1.7 pts
  M0197: cleared all 4: ... 3 of 4 folds, pick changed, 2009-2015 edge +7.0 pts
  M0243: cleared all 4: ... 3 of 4 folds, pick changed, 2009-2015 edge +1.9 pts
  M0258: cleared all 4: ... 3 of 4 folds, pick changed, 2009-2015 edge +2.3 pts
```

**Two of the four are artefacts of exactly the bug this section describes:**

| method | arm the signal read | edge | arm that would be promoted | edge | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M0092 | `M0092-SQRT-CAP10` | +1.73 | `M0092-SQRT-CAP10` — same arm | **+1.73** | genuine |
| M0197 | `M0197-Y20` | +7.02 | `M0197-Y20-SW` | **−3.34** | **artefact** |
| M0243 | `M0243-NEAR20-T3` (a **dose rung**) | +1.91 | `M0243-NEAR20-SW` | **−3.92** | **artefact** |
| M0258 | `M0258-M-B0` | +2.26 | `M0258-M-B0` — same arm | **+2.26** | genuine |

So the lab is, right now, printing *"This is the moment survivorship-free price history is worth
buying"* for two methods whose own promotable variant has a **negative** edge in the era the dev
store covers best. That is the precise failure the 4th condition exists to prevent, and it is
happening, not approaching.

Measured by phase 3's planner and verified here independently: fixing the column to read the
promoted variant changes the firing set from `{M0092, M0197, M0243, M0258}` to
`{M0072, M0092, M0258}` — M0197 and M0243 stop, and **M0072 starts**, because its hindsight arm
`M0072-Z2` reads −1.09 while its promoted `M0072-Z2-RAWRANK` reads +2.26. The fix is therefore
**not** a one-way tightening; it is a correction with a winner and two losers.

### Population note

This audit reproduces **74** gate refusals among **78** dev-eligible methods, matching the
handover exactly. Its kin-clean fold-refused count is **44**, against the handover's 45, with
29 at exactly 2 of 4 against the handover's 34, and **9** whose own promoted variant wins a
majority against the handover's 15. The handover's figures came from `lab status`; these come
from calling `hardgate.fold_record` / `variant_record` / `failed_kin` directly, and are verified
row-for-row against `lab walkforward`. The difference does not move any conclusion — on the
handover's own larger numbers the flip count would be 2 either way, because the flips are gated by
D11b, not by the population size.
