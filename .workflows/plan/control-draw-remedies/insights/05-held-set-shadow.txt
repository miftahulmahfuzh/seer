To draw a fair blind opponent, the lab has to know how many names the real book takes in
each month. It was estimating that by taking a fresh top-N at the previous month end and
asking how many of those names would have survived into this month. That stand-in is too
fresh: a just-taken top twenty is ranked 1 to 20, and a book that only drops a name once it
falls past rank 40 almost never drops one from there. A REAL held set has accumulated names
that drifted toward the edge, and it sheds them far faster.

Measured on the paper-gains book over 169 monthly decisions: the estimator read 304 entries
where the real book takes 624. Just under half.

The fix is to replay the book's own rule backwards a couple of steps instead of guessing -
reconstruct what it actually held at the previous decision. Two steps is enough and more
buys nothing: the estimate goes 0.72 of the truth at one step, 0.95 at two, and then wanders
inside 0.94 to 0.96 all the way out to eight steps. Two steps costs 305 bars of history
against a 473-bar ceiling, leaving 168 spare.

The 320 missing entries split three ways, and only one of the three is what anybody was
looking for:

- 44% was the market switch. This book sits in cash for 69 of the window's 238 months, and
  the old stand-in scored a phantom top twenty at the previous month end regardless. On the
  nine decisions that follow a cash month it read 38 entries where the truth is a complete
  180-name refill. A backward replay closes this for free, because the chain empties itself
  on a cash month exactly as the real book does. Nobody had separated this before and it is
  the largest single piece.
- 43% was the freshness problem the fix was proposed for.
- 13% is a different defect entirely, and no replay depth closes it: every book reads
  TODAY'S membership list when scoring an earlier date. It is worth 0.067 of ratio here and
  no more than 0.046 on any other book measured. It is carried forward unrepaired.

And the measurement that mattered most for cost: on eight of the nine books, the old
estimator was already right - ratios from 0.988 to 1.001, and for the four whose real books
cannot read their own holdings the replay returns identically 1.000 at every depth from one
to fifteen. The shadow was needed on exactly one book of nine. Measuring every book before
building anything is what kept this from being eight unnecessary re-draws.
