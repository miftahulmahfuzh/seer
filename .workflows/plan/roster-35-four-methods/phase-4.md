## Goal

`M0133-N40` — the up/down asymmetry rank over 40 names with the market switch **off** — is the one
book in this lab that earns its lead (+9.6%/yr funded against SPY TR's +7.2%) *and* is
behaviourally free of every test-failed book (0.57 / 0.59 / 0.58, far under the 0.85 kin bar). Its

argument, whether that fall can be cut by a brake that reads **the book's own account** instead of
the market — and whether such a brake leaves the kin reading clean, which every market-reading
brake in this lab does not.

The deliverable is one new, pre-registered method file with six variants, one `lab run`, two kin
readings taken in the right order, a verdict in the owner's language, and a dedicated test module

## Design decisions, argued before any code

These five are the whole of the engineering judgement in this phase. Each is a decision a reader
of the verdict must be able to check.

### D1 — The brake reads an **unbraked shadow**, not the braked account. (The phase's biggest call.)

M0109's docstring states the trap exactly: *"A brake that reads its own braked account is a
ratchet: once trimmed, the account stops falling, so its drawdown stops recovering and the
exposure never comes back."* It is worse than a one-way valve — a ratchet brake would read the
2008 trough, cut to cash, and then never see a recovery because a cash account has no drawdown to
recover from, so the book would sit in cash through 2009–2015, which is the exact stretch this
ranking exists to capture.

So M0149 does what M0109 does: `shadow_of` rebuilds **the ungated 40-name asymmetry book itself**
from bars — re-picking at every month-end mark of SPY's calendar and chaining the equal-weight
baskets — gross (no fees, no deposits, no cash drag), membership-free, and reads its drawdown from
a **trailing 252-session high**, not an inception high. The signal is then a pure function of bars,
which is what makes the P4 identity and the no-look-ahead check mean anything.

Two honest divergences, disclosed in the file's docstring and to be restated in the verdict:

1. **The shadow is membership-free.** Point-in-time membership reaches an allocator only through
   `prepare_market`, which this method does not use, so the shadow ranks every symbol in the
   store's price history (minus `f_factor.EXCLUDED`, which is `{"SPY"}`). The real book ranks
   point-in-time members. Where the two differ, the shadow is slightly broader. It is a signal,
   not an accounting.
2. **The shadow is gross and unfunded.** A deposit-fed account's own high is lifted by every
   top-up, which would make a brake read off the real account bite more often than any backtest
   suggests. Reading a gross index removes that distortion and means the same brake reads the same
   way at any account size.

`peak_n = 252` and `WARM = 504` are **M0109's measured values, reused unchanged and not
re-calibrated here**. M0109 measured on this store that a three-year high holds the 2000 top against
a book all through 2003–2007 and the 2007 top through 2010–2015, so a longer window would lose the
best-covered years by construction. Nothing about this method is calibrated on the dev window.

### D2 — The tape is `monthly-hold-frac-gotrade-tband`, and the method carries its own control.

A brake's whole mechanism is "set a lower target weight and let the engine trade to it". Under
`resize_basis="equity"` — `MONTHLY_HOLD_FRAC_GOTRADE`, which `M0133-N40` ran on — a held target is
traded only once its gap reaches `RESIZE_BAND` (1%) of the **whole account**
(`sim/book.py:_beyond_band`). At 40 names a slot is 2.5% of equity, so a trim fires only once
exposure drops to 0.60 or below; every exposure between 0.65 and 1.00 is computed and then never
executed. The brake would stop being a proportional dial and become a crude switch at
drawdown ≥ 10%, and a null result would be unreadable — "the brake does not help" and "the brake
never traded" would look identical. `seer-resize-band-never-fires` states the rule flatly: *never
design a method whose effect needs the equity-basis band to fire.*

Under `resize_basis="target"` the band is 20% of the holding's **own** target
(`TARGET_RESIZE_BAND`), so a trim fires once the braked weight is ~17% below the held value and
again every further ~20% down. That is a genuine proportional dial with a declared 20% deadband.
`MONTHLY_HOLD_FRAC_GOTRADE_TBAND` is otherwise byte-identical to `MONTHLY_HOLD_FRAC_GOTRADE`
(same cadence, fractional shares, Gotrade's real fees), is promotable, and raises **no owner
input** — eleven lab methods from M0123 on already use it.

Changing the tape means `M0133-N40`'s recorded curve is no longer the exact yardstick, so **variant
1 is an in-lab control on the same tape with both brakes off**. Under `DSR_POLICY = "methods"` an
extra variant costs the lab nothing in N (`seer-lab-n-policy-overcounts-looks`), so this is free
insurance and it buys two things: a same-tape yardstick for the five braked arms, and a direct
price of the tape change on this one book.

The tape change is expected to be small here and that is measured, not hoped: `M0133-N40`'s
recorded `turnover` is **5.662** (~47%/month) and its recorded `exposure` is **0.972**. Per
`seer-resize-band-never-fires` the deposit leak is set by turnover, and a book already 97% invested
has almost nothing to leak. So the control should land close to `M0133-N40`, and if it does not,
*that* is the finding and the braked arms are read against the control regardless.

**Idle cash earns nothing.** `MONTHLY_HOLD_FRAC_GOTRADE_TBAND_CASH` would credit the T-bill yield,
but it raises the owner input `cash-yield` (nobody has verified Gotrade pays interest on idle
dollars) and paper refuses it. A braked book holds a great deal of cash, so every braked number
here is a **floor**: a real account that earned anything on its waiting money would do better. That
sentence belongs in the verdict.

### D3 — The clock stays monthly.

The book engine reads an allocator on rank and resize sessions only, so the brake can be read no
faster than the resize cadence. The weekly alternative would change two things at once (the brake
and the clock) against a monthly control, and M0109 already measured that weekly reacts sooner and
is therefore wrong sooner while paying Gotrade's minimum fee on 40 small orders. One variable per
comparison: monthly, as `M0133-N40` ran.

### D4 — Import **free functions only**. Never compose another method's allocator object. ★

`method.config_text` writes every allocator — top level *and nested in params* — as `<ID>`
(`registry._canon`), and `hardgate._INGREDIENT` greps that text for `<M\d{4}>` to build the
ingredient half of the kin set (D12). An ingredient drags in **its own family and ancestors**.

`M0001`'s family is `stock-momentum-risk-managed`. **`M0002` is `test-failed` and sits in that same
family.** So wrapping M0001's `OWNVOL` allocator would make M0149 kin of `M0002` by D4+D12 — a
refusal recorded before a single number was measured, on the very method whose entire point is
that it is *not* kin of M0002.

Therefore the vol arms import `basket_scale`, the free function, and never `OWNVOL`. The same rule
covers `m0109` (import `exposure`, `braked`, `shadow_wealth`, `shadow_drawdown`, `WARM`; never
`BRAKE`) and `m0110` (`targets_from_scores`, `CapturePrepared`; never `CAPTURE`). The one allocator
object this file holds is `m0133`'s `ASYM`, and it is **called**, not nested in params — `ASYM` does
not appear in `_canon(params)` because `AccountBrakeParams` holds an `AsymParams`, which is a plain
dataclass. Verify after writing the file:

`idea` row) and its `parent_id` stays `M0133`. Ancestry is `M0133` (dev-eligible) → `M0110`
(rejected) → `M0108` (rejected). None is `test-failed`, so D4 is clean and the only live kin
question is D14, behaviour — which is exactly the question this method exists to answer.

### D5 — The base book is M0133's allocator, called directly.

`OwnAccountBrake.targets*` obtains its unbraked targets by calling `ASYM.targets*` with
`AsymParams(N40, gate="none")` — the literal params of `M0133-N40`. The brake is then the only
difference between this method and its parent, by construction rather than by inspection, and the
control arm's targets are provably identical to `M0133-N40`'s (test 1 in Step 3). Re-deriving the
rank in this file would have been a divergence risk for no gain, exactly as M0133 reused M0110's
`_participation` rather than re-deriving it.

`AccountBrakeParams.__post_init__` **refuses any `book.gate` but `"none"`**. The method's entire
claim is that it reads no market switch; making that a type error means no later amendment can
quietly reintroduce one.

### D6 — `lookback` declares what the **picks** need; the brake's depth is `WARM`. ★ measured

`OwnAccountBrake.lookback` returns `max(ASYM.lookback(book), vol_n + 1)` = **253**, and
deliberately does **not** include `WARM + peak_n`. This was checked rather than assumed, and the
check found the one place it shows:

`allocatorkit.assert_p4_identity` cuts every history to `lookback(p)` bars through `d` and asserts
the answer is unchanged. On a calendar deep enough for the brake to be live, a 253-bar cut is
shallower than `WARM` (504), so on the cut the brake correctly **stands down** and returns the
control's targets — and that assertion fails. Measured on a 2,200-session synthetic market while
this plan was written.

The alternative is worse and is what the memory warns about. An honest brake lookback is at least
`peak_n + factor_lookback` ≈ **505** bars, which breaches the smoke fixture's hard 473-bar cap
(`seer-lab-method-lookback-capped-by-smoke-fixture`): `run_candidate` would never accumulate enough
warmup, every candidate would target nothing, and `test_lab_methods.py::test_allocator_contract`'s
`assert nonempty > 0` would fail for all six. Shrinking `peak_n` to dodge that would be calibrating
a frozen, measured constant against a test fixture — the exact move the memory says not to make.

So this method takes M0109's documented position verbatim (*"what the PICKS need; the brake's own
depth is WARM and it stands down below it"*), and pays for it the way the memory prescribes — with
a dedicated test module rather than a shrunken window:

- `assert_p4_identity(..., check_lookback=False)` for the braked params, with the reason in the
  test's docstring;
- `assert_p4_identity(...)` with the check **on** for the control and the vol arm, whose depth
  genuinely is `vol_n + 1`;
- `assert_no_lookahead` runs in full for every arm — the direction that actually matters
  (never reading a bar dated on or after `d`) is never relaxed;
- plus `test_below_the_declared_lookback_the_brake_degrades_to_the_control`, which pins what the
  relaxed check gives up: below the declared depth the answer is the **control's**, never a third
  thing. That is a stronger statement than the check it replaces.

**Verified while planning (do not re-derive):** with this shape, all seven tests in Step 3 pass, the
shared contract in `test_lab_methods.py` passes for all six candidates on the smoke market
(`nonempty = 20` each), all six complete a smoke `run_candidate` (221 snapshots each), and
`ruff check` under the repo's exact ruleset (`select = ["E9","F"]`, `ignore = ["F401"]`) is clean
for both files.

`lab kin` calls `hardgate.kin_matrix`, which returns **every (dev variant × test-failed tested
variant) pair**, and `--all` prints every one. Read `M0133-N40`'s own row.

**The trap, and how this step avoids both halves of it**
(`seer-behavioural-kin-reports-only-the-best-twin`): `hardgate.behavioural_kin` — which is what
`lab status`, `lab gate` and the promote gate print — keeps **one `Twin` per failed method**, the
single best-correlating variant. Using it as a per-variant membership test is wrong in both
directions:
- a variant correlating 0.94 that loses to a sibling by 0.001 **never appears, and reads as clean**;
- every *other* variant **inherits a refusal computed for a book that is not it** — which is
  precisely what happened to `M0133-G40` and nearly cost a method id.

So: **never read a per-variant kin number from `lab status`, `lab gate` or `behavioural_kin`.**
`kin_matrix`/`lab kin --all` is the only per-variant source, and it is the same path the gate
takes, pair for pair, failing closed on a price-fingerprint mismatch exactly as the gate does.

And: **the bar is calibrated, not a gate detector** (`seer-d14-kin-bar-is-calibrated`). 24 random
40-name books behind the same switch never crossed 0.70 against `M0002-REL-85`, so 0.8964 for
`M0133-G40` is temperament, not a shared holdings list, and 0.57 for `M0133-N40` is genuine
behavioural freedom rather than an artefact of having no gate.

**Gate on this step.** Expect `M0133-N40` at roughly 0.57 / 0.59 / 0.58 against the four
test-failed books (`M0002-REL-85`, `M0021-B70-RAW`, `M0022-W-TV14`,
`M0029-B70-RAW-FRAC`). **If `M0133-N40`'s own row crosses 0.85 against any of them, stop.** The
door the whole method rests on is shut, nothing has been spent, and the right output is a
`lab note` on the `idea` row saying so plus a `lab drop`. Do not write the method file.

### Step 2: write the method file

**File:** `engine/src/seer_engine/lab/methods/m0149_own_account_brake.py` (new)

**Change:** the complete method. Prose docstring in m0133's and m0109's style, explaining *why*
each piece is shaped as it is. Hypothesis and expected-failure written now, before any number is
seen, and frozen by the commit in Step 4.

**Code:**

```python
"""M0149 — The asymmetry book kept invested, braked by its own falling account instead of the
market's trend.

Source: a variation of M0133. Its ungated arm, ``M0133-N40``, is the one book this lab holds that
is both *profitable* and *behaviourally free*: the up/down participation spread over 40 equal
slots with the market switch OFF earns +9.6% a year on the owner's deposits against SPY TR's
+7.2%, and its residual correlation against the four test-failed books reads 0.57 (M0002-REL-85),
0.59 (M0021) and 0.58 (F4-MOM12-N20-TREND) -- far under the 0.85 kin bar, where its gated sibling
``M0133-G40`` reads 0.8964 and is refused. Its only defect is the size of the fall: 69.1% peak to
trough, 2008 at -51.4%, against a 50% dev screen and the owner's 25% real-money bar.

WHY THE GATE CANNOT BE THE ANSWER. The obvious fix -- put the SPY 200-day switch back in front of
it -- is the fix that closed M0133. The lab's own measurement of that switch is that it earns its
whole twenty-year advantage in 2001, 2002 and 2008: it puts a book in cash in the same handful of
months every *other* book in this lab is in cash, because every other book reads the same market.
Hard-gate rule (D14) measures exactly that -- a method's monthly returns, de-funded and less its
own fit on SPY's, correlated against a test-failed book's -- and refuses on it. A market-reading
brake therefore buys a smaller fall with a refusal. This method asks whether a brake that reads
the BOOK can buy the same fall without one.

THE TWO BRAKES, both already implemented in this lab and imported rather than re-derived:

* **the own-drawdown brake** (M0109). Exposure is ``1 - dd / taper``, clipped into
  ``[floor, 1]`` and rounded to the nearest 0.05, where ``dd`` is how far the book sits below its
  own trailing 252-session high: full exposure at the high, all cash by the time it is down
  ``taper``, a straight line between. ``M0109.exposure`` and ``M0109.braked`` are imported whole.
* **the own-volatility target** (M0001/M0002). Every weight is multiplied by
  ``min(1, target_vol / the basket's own annualized 63-day volatility)``. ``M0001.basket_scale``
  is imported whole. It is one-sided: it de-risks and never levers.

Neither reads a price of SPY's, a breadth count or a term structure. Both de-risk on a schedule
set by this book's own path, which is the schedule the failed books do not share -- that is the
whole hypothesis, and D14 is what will judge it.

THE SHADOW, and the ratchet it exists to avoid. An allocator sees bars, members, the data date and
what the book holds; it never sees the account's equity curve, so "how far is this book below its
own high" must be rebuilt from bars. ``shadow_of`` rebuilds it the way M0109 does: re-pick THIS
book -- the ungated 40-name asymmetry rank -- at every month-end mark of SPY's calendar and chain
the equal-weight baskets day by day. Four things about that reconstruction, because they are what
could fool us:

  * It is the UNBRAKED book, deliberately, and this is the single most important decision in the
    method. A brake that reads its own BRAKED account is a ratchet: once trimmed, the account
    stops falling, so its drawdown stops recovering and the exposure never comes back. Here that
    failure would be total -- the brake would cut in 2008 and then hold a cash account, which has
    no drawdown to recover from, straight through 2009-2015, which is the stretch this ranking
    exists to capture. Reading the unbraked shadow asks the question the brake actually wants
    answered -- "how far is this kind of book below its high" -- and re-enters when the book's
    own kind of names recover rather than when the trimmed account does.
  * It is MEMBERSHIP-FREE. Point-in-time membership reaches an allocator only through
    ``prepare_market``, which this method does not use, so the shadow ranks every symbol in the
    store's price history except ``f_factor.EXCLUDED``. Those symbols ARE the index members, past
    and present; where the shadow and the book differ, the shadow is slightly broader. It is a
    signal, not an accounting.
  * It is GROSS: no fees, no deposits, no cash drag, no dividends. A deposit-funded account's own
    high is lifted by every top-up, which would make a brake read off the real account bite more
    often than any backtest suggests. A gross index removes that entirely and means the same brake
    reads the same way at any account size.
  * The running high is TRAILING over ``peak_n`` sessions, never since inception, and it needs
    ``WARM`` sessions of calendar before the brake may act at all. Both numbers are M0109's,
    imported and NOT re-calibrated here: M0109 measured on this store that a three-year high holds
    the 2000 top against a book all through 2003-2007 and the 2007 top through 2010-2015, so a
    longer window would be guaranteed to lose the best-covered years before the run began. Where
    the ``WARM`` depth is absent the brake stands down and the variant IS its control -- a hard
    on/off on a fixed session count, never a shortened window, so the P4 identity holds either way.

THE TAPE, and why it is not the parent's. ``M0133-N40`` ran on ``monthly-hold-frac-gotrade``, where
the rebalance band is 1% of the WHOLE account. At 40 names a slot is 2.5% of equity, so a trim is
executed only once exposure falls to 0.60 or below and every exposure between 0.65 and 1.00 is
computed and then never traded -- the dial would silently become a switch, and a null result would
not be readable. This method runs on ``monthly-hold-frac-gotrade-tband``, where the band is 20% of
the holding's OWN target, so the dial executes as a dial with a declared 20% deadband. Everything
else about the tape is the parent's: monthly, fractional shares, Gotrade's real fees, the owner's
real deposits. ``N40-RAW`` is the paired in-lab control on that same tape with both brakes off, so
the five braked arms are read against a yardstick that pays the same trading costs; it also prices
the tape change on this book, which ``M0133-N40``'s recorded turnover of 5.66 and exposure of 0.972
say should be small.

IDLE CASH EARNS NOTHING here. The cash-crediting tape raises the owner input ``cash-yield`` (that
Gotrade pays interest on idle dollars is not verified) and paper refuses it. A braked book holds a
great deal of cash, so every braked return below is a FLOOR, not an estimate.

Assumptions, written before the run:

- The idea named five arms (drawdown cuts at 15% and 25%, own vol at 14% and 16%, and both). The
  sixth slot goes to the in-lab control rather than to a sixth dial, for the tape reason above.
  Under ``DSR_POLICY = "methods"`` a sixth variant costs the lab nothing in N, so the control is
  free and the comparison is airtight instead of nearly so.
- The combined arm pairs the GENTLER drawdown taper with the TIGHTER vol target (``DD25-TV14``)
  rather than stacking two aggressive dials. The arm's question is whether the two brakes de-risk
  on different occasions or the same ones; two maximal dials would answer only "yes, a book held
  mostly in cash falls less".
- The drawdown dial is stepped to the nearest 0.05 because M0109's ``exposure`` is and it is
  imported unchanged; the volatility dial is not stepped because M0001's ``basket_scale`` is not.
  Both are the lab's existing implementations, run as they stand. The tape's 20% deadband is the
  anti-churn device here, and adding a second, undeclared one would be a dial invented after the
  fact.
- ``book.gate`` must be ``"none"``: the method's whole claim is that it reads no market switch, so
  a gated configuration is refused by the params type rather than left to care.
- ``M0109.exposure`` and ``M0109.shadow_drawdown`` read only ``taper``, ``floor`` and ``peak_n``
  from the params they are handed. ``AccountBrakeParams`` carries those three under exactly those
  names, so M0109's implementations run on it unchanged and there is one definition of the dial in
  the lab rather than two. M0109's file is frozen against its recorded sha, so the coupling cannot
  drift.
- Only free functions are imported from the brake and vol-scaling methods; no other method's
  allocator object is ever nested in these params. A nested allocator is written into
  ``config_text`` as ``<MNNNN>`` and makes that method an INGREDIENT (hard gate D12), dragging in
  its family and ancestors -- and M0001's family, ``stock-momentum-risk-managed``, is the
  test-failed M0002's family. Composing it would have refused this method on kin before a single
  number was measured.
- Participation, screen, weighting and pricing are the parent's exactly: ``ASYM.targets`` is
  called with ``AsymParams(N40, gate="none")``, the literal params of ``M0133-N40``, so the brake
  is the only difference between this method and its parent by construction.
- Reads only bars dated <= ``data_date``. Pure: no clock, no I/O, no randomness.
"""

@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)
class AccountBrakeParams:
    """``book`` is the unbraked book; the rest is the two brakes that read it and nothing else.

    ``taper``/``floor``/``peak_n`` carry M0109's names because M0109's ``exposure`` and
    ``shadow_drawdown`` are handed this object and read exactly those three attributes.
    ``taper=None`` and ``target_vol=None`` together are the control: full exposure, always.
    """

    book: AsymParams
    taper: Decimal | None = None  # shadow drawdown at which exposure reaches `floor`; None = off
    floor: Decimal = Decimal("0")  # the least exposure the taper reaches
    peak_n: int = 252  # sessions the shadow's running high is taken over (about one year)
    target_vol: Decimal | None = None  # annualized own-vol target; None = off
    vol_n: int = 63  # daily returns in the volatility estimate (63 = one quarter)

    def __post_init__(self) -> None:
        if not isinstance(self.book, AsymParams):
            raise TypeError(f"book must be AsymParams, got {type(self.book).__name__}")
        if self.book.gate != "none":
            raise ValueError(
                f"book.gate must be 'none': M0149 brakes on the account, never on the market, "
                f"got {self.book.gate!r}"
            )
        if self.taper is not None:
            if not isinstance(self.taper, Decimal) or not self.taper.is_finite():
                raise TypeError(f"taper must be None or a Decimal, got {self.taper!r}")
            if not 0 < self.taper <= 1:
                raise ValueError(f"taper must be in (0, 1], got {self.taper}")
        if not isinstance(self.floor, Decimal) or not self.floor.is_finite():
            raise TypeError(f"floor must be a Decimal, got {self.floor!r}")
        if not 0 <= self.floor < 1:
            raise ValueError(f"floor must be a Decimal in [0, 1), got {self.floor}")
        if isinstance(self.peak_n, bool) or not isinstance(self.peak_n, int) or self.peak_n < 20:
            raise ValueError(f"peak_n must be an int >= 20, got {self.peak_n!r}")
        if self.target_vol is not None:
            if not isinstance(self.target_vol, Decimal) or not self.target_vol.is_finite():
                raise TypeError(f"target_vol must be None or a Decimal, got {self.target_vol!r}")
            if self.target_vol <= 0:
                raise ValueError(f"target_vol must be > 0, got {self.target_vol}")
        if isinstance(self.vol_n, bool) or not isinstance(self.vol_n, int) or self.vol_n < 20:
            raise ValueError(f"vol_n must be an int >= 20, got {self.vol_n!r}")

    def as_dict(self) -> dict[str, str]:
        out = {
            "taper": "none" if self.taper is None else str(self.taper),
            "floor": str(self.floor),
            "peak_n": str(self.peak_n),
            "target_vol": "none" if self.target_vol is None else str(self.target_vol),
            "vol_n": str(self.vol_n),
        }
        for key, v in self.book.as_dict().items():
            out[f"book.{key}"] = v
        return out


def _check(params: object) -> AccountBrakeParams:
    if not isinstance(params, AccountBrakeParams):
        raise TypeError(f"params must be AccountBrakeParams, got {type(params).__name__}")
    return params


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- the shadow


def shadow_of(
    rows_at: Callable[[date], list[FactorRow]], grid: ReturnGrid, book: AsymParams
) -> np.ndarray:
    """The UNBRAKED asymmetry book's gross wealth index over ``grid.dates``, starting at 1.0.

    ``rows_at`` yields the screened rows at a date (``factor_rows`` on the single-window path,
    ``FactorPrepared.rows_on`` on the prepared one), so both paths share this one function and
    cannot disagree. The picks are taken through ``targets_from_scores`` -- M0110's selection, the
    same call the real book makes -- rather than by re-sorting the scores here, so the shadow's
    basket and the book's basket can never drift apart on a tie-break or a coverage floor.

    ``shadow_wealth`` is M0109's: it re-picks at every month-end mark and chains the equal-weight
    baskets. Entry t depends only on marks before t and returns at or before t, so the entries at
    or before any date d are the same whether the grid was built from the whole history or from
    history cut at d -- which is what makes the P4 identity hold with the brake live.
    """

    def picks_at(d: date) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        rows = rows_at(d)
        if not rows:
            return ()
        scores = asym_scores(grid, [r.symbol for r in rows], d, book)
        return tuple(t.symbol for t in targets_from_scores(rows, scores, book))

    return shadow_wealth(grid, picks_at)


def account_share(
    history: Mapping[str, History],
    grid: ReturnGrid | None,
    wealth: np.ndarray | None,
    base: tuple[Target, ...],
    data_date: date,
    p: AccountBrakeParams,
) -> Decimal:
    """The share of the account both brakes together allow at ``data_date``.

    The drawdown dial is M0109's ``exposure`` of M0109's ``shadow_drawdown`` (1 when ``taper`` is
    None, when the shadow is unmeasurable, or when the calendar is shallower than ``WARM``). The
    volatility dial is M0001's ``basket_scale``, which returns None when it would not cut -- it
    de-risks and never levers. They multiply, and the order does not matter: ``basket_scale``
    normalizes the basket's weights to 1 before measuring it, so the vol reading is the same
    whether it sees the braked targets or the unbraked ones, and it is handed the unbraked ones so
    the two dials stay independently readable.
    """
    share = exposure(shadow_drawdown(grid, wealth, data_date, p), p)
    if p.target_vol is None:
        return share
    vol = basket_scale(history, base, data_date, p.vol_n, p.target_vol)
    return share if vol is None else share * vol


# --------------------------------------------------------------------------- the allocator


@dataclass
class BrakedBookPrepared:
    """M0133's prepared columns, plus one unbraked shadow index per distinct book shape.

    The shadow is keyed on the whole ``AsymParams`` -- the screen, the window, the market and the
    book size -- and NOT on the brake, because the shadow is the unbraked book and must read the
    same for every taper. The dedicated test asserts that directly.
    """

    book: Any = field(repr=False)  # m0110.CapturePrepared, as M0133's prepare() returns it
    shadows: dict[AsymParams, np.ndarray | None] = field(default_factory=dict, repr=False)

    @property
    def history(self) -> Mapping[str, History]:
        return self.book.history

    def shadow(self, p: AsymParams) -> np.ndarray | None:
        if p not in self.shadows:
            grid = self.book.grid(p.market)
            if grid is None:
                self.shadows[p] = None
            else:
                universe = frozenset(self.book.history)
                self.shadows[p] = shadow_of(
                    lambda d: self.book.factor.rows_on(universe, d, p.inner), grid, p
                )
        return self.shadows[p]


class OwnAccountBrake:
    """M0133's ungated asymmetry book, trimmed by how far that book is below its own high.

    Held symbols get no special treatment: every rank session is a fresh top set and every resize
    session re-reads both dials, so a name that leaves the chosen set is signal-exited by the book
    engine. ``held`` is passed through to M0133's allocator unchanged and, with ``gate="none"``,
    is not read by it.
    """

    id = "M0149"

    def lookback(self, params: Any) -> int:
        p = _check(params)
        # What the PICKS need, from the parent, plus the volatility estimate's own depth. The
        # shadow's depth is WARM and it stands down below it, exactly as M0109's does, so it adds
        # nothing here and the smoke fixture's 473-bar cap is not approached.
        return max(ASYM.lookback(p.book), p.vol_n + 1)

    def symbols(self, params: Any) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        return tuple(ASYM.symbols(_check(params).book))

    def holds(self, params: Any) -> tuple[str, ...]:
        _check(params)
        return ()

    def uses_members(self, params: Any) -> bool:
        _check(params)
        return True

    def targets(self, history: Mapping[str, History], members: AbstractSet[str], data_date: date,
                held: frozenset[str], params: Any) -> tuple[Target, ...]:
        p = _check(params)
        base = ASYM.targets(history, members, data_date, held, p.book)
        if not base or (p.taper is None and p.target_vol is None):
            return base
        grid = build_grid(history, p.book.market)
        wealth = None
        if grid is not None and p.taper is not None:
            universe = frozenset(history)
            wealth = shadow_of(
                lambda d: factor_rows(history, universe, d, p.book.inner), grid, p.book
            )
        return braked(base, account_share(history, grid, wealth, base, data_date, p))

    def prepare(self, history: Mapping[str, History]) -> BrakedBookPrepared:
        return BrakedBookPrepared(ASYM.prepare(history))

    def targets_prepared(self, prepared: Any, members: AbstractSet[str], data_date: date,
                         held: frozenset[str], params: Any) -> tuple[Target, ...]:
        if not isinstance(prepared, BrakedBookPrepared):
            raise TypeError(f"prepared must be BrakedBookPrepared, got {type(prepared).__name__}")
        p = _check(params)
        base = ASYM.targets_prepared(prepared.book, members, data_date, held, p.book)
        if not base or (p.taper is None and p.target_vol is None):
            return base
        grid = prepared.book.grid(p.book.market)
        wealth = prepared.shadow(p.book) if p.taper is not None else None
        return braked(
            base, account_share(prepared.history, grid, wealth, base, data_date, p)
        )
```

## Rollback

The lab is append-only by trigger: a trial row cannot be deleted, a method's status moves only
forward, and `analysis` may only grow. "Rolling back" this phase means **writing a further note**,
never editing a row — that is the point of the lab, and it is what the plan index says.

Concretely:

- **Before Step 4 (the pre-registration commit):** delete the two new files. Nothing has been
  spent; the `M0149` row is still `idea`. If Step 1's kin reading closed the door, record that with
  `lab note M0149 --verdict "<plain words>"` and `lab drop M0149`, and this phase ends there.
- **Between Step 4 and Step 6:** `git revert` the pre-registration commit. No trial exists, the
  method row is still `idea`, the lab's N has not moved.
- **After Step 6:** nothing is recoverable and nothing should be. The six trials stay, the method's
  status is `dev-eligible` or `rejected`, and the lab's N is permanently one method higher. If the
  method file itself turns out to be wrong, the remedy is a dated note on the method saying what
  was wrong and why the recorded numbers cannot be read, plus — if the question is still worth
  asking — a new variation id. `git revert` on the file would only break
  `test_lab_methods.py`'s sha check, which exists precisely to stop that.
- **The test module** (`engine/tests/test_m0149_own_account_brake.py`) is ordinary code and can be
  reverted or amended freely at any time; it is not part of the pre-registered record.
- Nothing in this phase is a schema change, touches `db/migrations/`, touches the roster, or is
  destructive to any other phase's work. The only shared artifact is the lab pair, and Step 9's
  lock plus the no-rebase rule is what keeps it safe.
