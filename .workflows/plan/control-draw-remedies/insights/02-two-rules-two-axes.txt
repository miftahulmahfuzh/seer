A fair opponent for a strategy has to match it on two separate things, and the lab had been
trying to buy both with one rule. It cannot be done, and this set measured why three
different ways.

The lab's matched draw is a rule about NAMES: given a real book, it works out how many names
that book takes in each month and hands the blind opponent the same amount of churn. The
original requirement asked for one reusable rule of that kind to fix everything. Here is why
one rule was never available.

FIRST, how much money a book moves is not a question about names at all. The equal-weight
version of the top fifty holds the identical fifty names and records the identical 360
trades as the size-weighted version, and still moves 1.6 times as much money. No rule
deciding WHICH names an opponent holds can reach that, however cleverly the churn is
derived.

SECOND, a shared piece of code cannot work out the weights. Every book computes its own
slots its own way - one by company size under a ceiling, one by size under a different
ceiling, one by sizing each company separately and splitting share classes. A shared rule
has no way to produce any of those. What can be shared is the ASSIGNMENT: the book hands
over its own list of slot sizes with the names stripped off, and the shared rule decides
which of the opponent's names gets which slot.

THIRD, and this one is new and was measured rather than argued: the books do not even agree
on which direction needs fixing. On the top-fifty-by-size book, the blind opponent was
handing a brand-new name almost twice the slot the real book gives a brand-new name, and
that one fact accounted for essentially all of its excess trading. On the bond-like
size-weighted book, the opponent's new-name slot was already right - 0.97 of the book's. So
a rule that repairs the first HARMS the second: applied there it takes a compliant opponent
from 0.80 to 0.74 and pushes it out of the band it was already inside. A weight rule's scope
is per-book by measurement, not only by construction.

What DOES work, and shipped: one shared assignment contract, with each book supplying its
own slot list - the same arrangement the name rule already has, where five different books
each hand it their own slot count, sector cap or keep band. By the standard already in force
in that module, one shared contract plus a thin per-book adapter IS one reusable rule. On
the book it was built for it took the opponent's money movement from 1.90 times the book's
to 1.011 times - closing 98.8% of the excess, on identical names and within 1.003 of
identical trades.

So the honest shape of the answer is two rules on two axes: a name rule and a weight rule,
in two modules. A future set should not spend effort trying to collapse them into one.
