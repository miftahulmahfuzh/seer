A control is always drawn and run LATER than the book it controls - often weeks later,
sometimes months. The research store is rebuilt in between. So by default the lab compares a
control measured against one vintage of the price data with a book measured against another,
and calls the ratio a shape check.

How widespread: twelve distinct research-store fingerprints across the 862 recorded trials,
and ten of the fifteen recorded book-against-control comparisons cross a boundary. This is
structural, not an accident of one batch.

The important part is what it costs, because that was measured on four books this week
rather than assumed, and the answer is that it varies enormously:

- On the paper-gains book: EXACTLY ZERO. Re-running the book and both its controls in one
  sweep on one store reproduced every recorded figure to every digit, money included - the
  book's return came back as 0.1387873198052101 against a recorded 0.13878731980521. That
  book names only one market series and reads its members through a screen, which is about
  as insensitive to a store rebuild as a book gets.
- On the bond-like size-weighted book: 0.0067 of ratio - and it flipped a verdict. That
  book's control was published as missing the match band by 0.0059. Re-measured in one
  sweep it reads 0.8008 and is INSIDE the band. The "near miss" was measurement error, not
  draw error. It holds its names directly and it sat on a band edge, which is exactly where
  a boundary is decisive.
- On the top-fifty-by-size book: 0.22 points of MONEY. The book's recorded return is 5.63%
  a year on one store and 5.85% re-run on the current one. No verdict turned on it, but a
  published margin would move by that much depending on which is quoted.
- On its repaired twin: the whole of the difference. The twin exists to measure a share-count
  repair, and the two books' recorded returns differ - 5.63% against 5.85%. On the shared
  current store they are identical to four decimal places on every column: same trades, same
  turnover, same money. The difference still printed between those two pages is a difference
  between two vintages of the DATA, not between two books. A reader subtracting one page's
  figure from the other's is measuring a store rebuild.

So the rule is narrower than "every cross-store number is wrong", and the narrow version is
the one that survives:

    A shape ratio or a margin computed across two store fingerprints is not a comparison
    until it has been re-measured in one sweep. Whether re-measuring moves it is
    book-dependent and must be measured, not assumed.

And the reason this is worth more than the one verdict it moved: the store fingerprint is
recorded on every single trial, so a cross-boundary comparison is MECHANICALLY DETECTABLE.
The shape check could refuse one, or flag it, instead of quietly reporting it as a number.
That is a change to the shape checker and a plan set of its own - it was deliberately not
attempted here.
